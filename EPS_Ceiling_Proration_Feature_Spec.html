Status: Implemented, unit tested, ready for QA

1. Why this change

Every month the employer files an ECR (Electronic Challan cum Return) with EPFO. EPFO expects an employee's pension (EPS) wage to fit the days the employee actually contributed. Our rule caps it at:

EPS wages  ≤  EPS ceiling × (calendar days − NCP days) / calendar days

EPFO's published ECR rules require that EPS wages do not exceed EPF wages and that EPS remitted does not exceed 8.33% of EPS wages. The NCP-based cap above is our interpretation (see section 9).

  • EPS ceiling is ₹15,000 (₹25,000 from 17-Sep-2026, the confirmed effective date of the revision).
  • NCP days (Non-Contributory Period) are the days in the month the employee was not paid. They reduce the employee's pensionable service.

4. Business rules

4.1 NCP days

effective paid days = paid days + additional earning days

if effective paid days = 0:
    NCP = calendar days                  (nothing paid)
else:
    NCP = working days − effective paid days   (never below 0, rounded to a whole day)
    NCP is capped at calendar days − 1   (at least one EPS contributory calendar day when anything is paid)
  • Exception, nothing paid: when no wages are paid for the whole month, NCP = calendar days (e.g. 31 in October), not working days. In every other case NCP is counted in working days.
  • NCP is rounded to the nearest whole day, halves rounded up (ECR accepts whole days only): 0.5 day of LOP gives NCP 1, 0.25 day gives NCP 0.
  • NCP = LOP days from attendance plus working days before the date of joining or after the date of exit. Non-working days (weekly offs, holidays) are never paid or unpaid days, so they do not count as NCP, unless an unpaid leave is applied on them (it is then an LOP day).
  • NCP means unpaid working days for payroll purposes, including working days before the date of joining or after the date of exit (wages are not due for them, EPFO's NCP definition). This matches how wages are paid (monthly wage × paid days ÷ working days). Non-working calendar days are never NCP unless an unpaid leave is applied on them.

4.2 EPS wage

reduced EPS ceiling = ceiling × EPS contributory calendar days / calendar days   (rounded down to the rupee)
EPS wage            = lower of (employer EPF wage, reduced EPS ceiling)
EPS                 = 8.33% of EPS wage
EPF A/c 1           = employer 12% − EPS
  • Employer EPF wage (the starting point for EPS and EDLI):
    • Unrestricted: actual EPF wage × paid days ÷ working days (e.g. 78,266.50 × 24/26 = 72,246).
    • Restricted, NC off: cap × paid days ÷ working days.
    • Restricted, NC on: lower of the cap and (actual EPF wage × paid days ÷ working days).
    • Additional earning days are added on top: cap (or actual wage if unrestricted) × additional days ÷ working days. This extra is contributed to employee PF, A/c 1 and admin; EPS stays at its ceiling and EDLI at the full ceiling.
  • When EPS is not applicable (EPS off and not mandatory), EPS wage and EPS are 0 and the full 12% goes to A/c 1. NCP is still reported.
  • The "low earner, EPS mandatory" check still uses the full monthly wage against the full ceiling. LOP never changes who is a mandatory EPS member.

4.3 EDLI wage

EDLI wage = lower of (employer EPF wage, full ceiling)
EDLI      = 0.5% of EDLI wage
  • The EPF wage is already prorated for LOP, so EDLI follows LOP through the wage.
  • Unlike EPS, the ceiling is not reduced for NCP days: EDLI is insurance cover and is not linked to pensionable service days.

4.3a Admin charges

Admin = 0.5% of employer EPF wage     — no ceiling
  • Unchanged by this feature. Follows whatever wage the employer PF is paid on.

4.4 September 2026 (ceiling change on 17-Sep)

  • Each employed day uses the ceiling in force that day: ₹15,000 for 1–16 Sep, ₹25,000 from 17 Sep. An employee who joins on or after 17 Sep is on ₹25,000 for every employed day (EPFO FAQ Q7 Scenario A); a leaver before 17 Sep is on ₹15,000 only.
  • For a full-month employee the two parts weigh 16/30 and 14/30.
  • Restricted wage: each part is capped at its own ceiling, then blended. Full month, no LOP: 15,000 × 16/30 + 25,000 × 14/30 = 19,666.67, the full-month September blended ceiling (not a statutory ceiling).
    • Example, ₹20,000 restricted with NC on, 24 of 26 days paid: earned 20,000 × 24/26 = 18,461.54. 1–16 Sep: min(15,000, 18,461.54) = 15,000. 17–30 Sep: min(25,000, 18,461.54) = 18,461.54. Blended: 15,000 + 3,461.54 × 14/30 = 16,615.38.
  • EPS wage is worked out per part, then blended: in each part, EPS wage = lower of (that part's EPF wage, that part's reduced ceiling); the two results are blended by employed days. The 4.2 formula therefore applies to each part separately in September, not to the month's blended totals.
  • Reduced EPS ceiling: each part's ceiling is reduced for the same NCP days and rounded down first, then blended. NCP 2: floor(15,000 × 28/30) = 14,000 and floor(25,000 × 28/30) = 23,333 → 14,000 + 9,333 × 14/30 = 18,355.40 (slightly below 19,666.67 × 28/30 = 18,355.56 because of the per-part rounding).
  • The blend follows EPFO FAQ Q7 (each ceiling applies to its own days). EPFO has said September portal validations may be relaxed, so the portal's exact September check is not confirmed.

4.5 Payruns calculated before this release

  • Old payruns are not rewritten.
  • If an old payrun with LOP still has EPS on the full ceiling, ECR reports it the way the earlier ECR did: NCP 0 and the EPS wage on which EPS was remitted, so the line is not rejected and EPS wage matches EPS remitted. This is detected when:
    • the stored EPS wage is above the reduced EPS ceiling, or
    • no EPS wage is stored and the stored EPS contribution is above 8.33% of the reduced ceiling.
  • For September 2026 the reduced ceiling is blended over the employee's employed days (as payroll does), so new September payruns, including joiners after 17-Sep, keep their real NCP.
  • Old September 2026 payruns stored without EPS/EPF wage bases: ECR fills the bases from payroll but always reports the recorded contribution amounts (as deducted). If the recorded EPS is above 8.33% of the filled (NCP-reduced) EPS base, the row is treated as legacy: EPS wage on the unreduced base, NCP 0.
  • Every such line is recorded in the server log with a note to recalculate the payrun before filing.
  • Recalculating the payrun applies the new rules.
  • September 2026: payruns calculated before this release should be recalculated before the September ECR is filed.

5. When will the EPS wage visibly go down?

The rule runs for everyone, but it only lowers the number when the employer EPF wage after LOP is still above the reduced ceiling.

Employer setupEPS wage reduced with LOP?Why
Restricted + NC on, wage above the capYesEPF wage stays at the cap, ceiling is reduced
Unrestricted employer, wage above the ceilingYesEPF wage is the actual prorated wage, well above the ceiling
Restricted + NC offUsually noEPF wage already drops by paid ÷ working days, which is more than the ceiling reduction
Low earner (wage ≤ ceiling)Usually noSame reason
Additional days ≥ LOPNoNCP is 0

In all cases NCP days are still reported in ECR.

6.4 Checks on every ECR line

  1. EPS wage ≤ EPF wage.
  2. EPS wage ≤ ceiling × (calendar days − NCP) / calendar days. For September use the September blended ceiling for the employee's employed days (19,666.67 for a full month); small differences of a few paise come from rounding each part down first (see 4.4).
  3. EDLI wage = lower of the EPF wage (after LOP) and the full ceiling; its ceiling is not reduced for NCP days.
  4. A/c 1 + EPS = employer 12% (equals EE PF when employee and employer are both restricted).
  5. NCP = calendar days only when nothing is paid.
  6. Net pay and employee PF are the same as before this change for the same LOP.

8. Scope

  • Monthly ECR only. The ECR report covers regular monthly payruns. Full & final payruns are not included in the monthly ECR (including them was a bug); full & final filing is handled separately.
  • Out of scope: automatically recalculating payruns calculated before this release. Recalculate them before their ECR is filed (see 4.5).

9. Interpretations (no explicit EPFO rule)

These are product decisions. EPFO has not published a rule for them; they are documented so they are not mistaken for statutory requirements.

DecisionWhat we do
EPS ceiling reduced for NCP daysEPS wage capped at ceiling × (calendar days − NCP) / calendar days; the surplus goes to EPF A/c 1
NCP counted in working daysNCP = working days − paid days, consistent with how wages are paid; then subtracted from calendar days for the EPS cap
Days outside employment are NCPWorking days before the date of joining or after the date of exit are counted as NCP (wages are not due for them). Counted in working days, so a joiner's EPS contributory calendar days can be a few days more than their employed calendar days (weekends before joining are not added)
NCP roundingNearest whole day, halves rounded up (EPFO only requires whole days)
Additional earning daysCount as paid days (reduce NCP) and are contributed on top of the cap

No open questions.

10. FAQ

Does the employee's take-home pay change? No. Employee PF and net pay are unchanged. Only the employer split between EPS (pension) and EPF A/c 1 changes.

Does the employer pay more or less? Neither. The employer's total 12% is unchanged; a smaller EPS share moves into A/c 1.

Why is the EDLI ceiling not reduced for NCP days? EDLI is life-insurance cover and is not linked to pensionable service days. The EDLI wage still follows the EPF wage after LOP; only its ceiling stays at the full ₹15,000 / ₹25,000.

What if an employee had no pay at all? The ECR line shows NCP equal to the days in the month and all wages 0.

Do additional earning days matter? Yes. They count as paid days, so they reduce (or cancel) NCP.
